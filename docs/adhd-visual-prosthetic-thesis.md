# ADHD as a Continuity Problem

## A thesis for AR as a visual prosthetic

### TL;DR

ADHD is often framed as a motivation problem, a discipline problem, or a willpower problem.

That is not the most useful frame.

A better frame is this:

**ADHD is, in part, a continuity failure problem.**

The issue is not always knowing what matters.
The issue is keeping what matters perceptually alive long enough for action to remain aligned with intention.

In that sense, AR glasses may become more than a display layer or convenience device.
They may become a **visual prosthetic for priority persistence**.

---

## The core idea

For many people with ADHD, the main objective does not remain stably present in working consciousness.

The mind does not only get distracted by trivial things.
It gets captured by whatever becomes most immediate, most stimulating, most novel, or most visible.

This creates a recurring pattern:

1. The person knows the real priority.
2. Attention drifts.
3. A lower-value but more immediate task captures the system.
4. Time passes under false importance.
5. The person "returns" and realizes they were not acting on the true objective.

This is not merely forgetfulness.
It is a breakdown in **priority persistence**.

If the problem is partly perceptual, then part of the solution may also be perceptual.

---

## Why vision matters

Attention is heavily shaped by what remains in the visual field.

What stays visible has a structural advantage over what must be re-remembered.
A priority that must be mentally reloaded is fragile.
A priority that is continuously and intelligently present in the line of sight is more stable.

This suggests a simple but powerful hypothesis:

**If the most important task, constraint, or objective could remain perceptually present at the edge of awareness, some forms of ADHD-related drift may be reduced.**

Not cured.
Reduced.

The mechanism is not magical.
It is environmental scaffolding applied directly to the attentional bottleneck.

---

## AR glasses as a prosthetic layer

Most productivity tools assume the user will remember to check them.

That assumption breaks under attentional volatility.

Phones, task managers, notes apps, and calendar alerts all rely on re-entry:

- open the app
- remember the context
- reconstruct the priority
- resume motion

For people with ADHD, that reload cost is often exactly where continuity breaks.

AR changes the interface model.

Instead of requiring repeated re-entry, the system can place the correct cue directly into perception.

That means AR glasses could function as a prosthetic layer for:

- main objective persistence
- task hierarchy visibility
- interruption recovery
- anti-drift signaling
- contextual reminders at the moment of deviation

The point is not to fill vision with noise.
The point is to create a **minimal visual anchor** that protects the top priority from disappearing.

---

## Prosthetic, not enhancement

The word *prosthetic* matters.

A prosthetic does not make someone superhuman.
It compensates for a recurring functional gap.

In this case, the gap is not intelligence.
It is not effort.
It is not moral weakness.

The gap is the unstable persistence of the correct target inside consciousness.

So the proposition is not:

"AR glasses make people more productive."

The proposition is:

**For some ADHD users, AR glasses may serve as an attentional prosthetic by keeping the correct target visually available when internal continuity fails.**

That is a much stronger idea.

---

## A simple operating model

An ADHD visual prosthetic could work through a few basic rules.

### 1. One objective, always visible
A single line in the display:

- current objective
- current highest-value action
- current anti-goal

Not ten tasks.
Not a dashboard.
One live anchor.

### 2. Drift detection
If the user opens unrelated apps, changes context too often, stalls, or loops, the system infers drift.

### 3. Re-entry prompts
When drift is detected, the display surfaces a tiny corrective cue:

- `Main objective: finish X`
- `This is not the priority`
- `Return to the next smallest move`

### 4. State-aware adaptation
If the user is depleted, overwhelmed, or fragmented, the system changes the prompt depth.

Not:
- "Finish the project"

But:
- "Open the file"
- "Write one sentence"
- "Send one reply"

### 5. Constraint visibility
The display can also keep a live constraint present:

- do not switch tasks
- no new tabs
- finish before exploring
- stay with the bottleneck

This is less about reminder technology and more about **real-time cognitive stabilization**.

---

## What this solves

This kind of system may help with:

- losing the real priority after interruptions
- false urgency replacing real importance
- hyperfixating on low-output side quests
- context collapse between intention and action
- friction of reloading the task stack from memory

In plain English:

It may help the user keep the true thing visible long enough to act on it.

---

## What it does not solve

This thesis should not be exaggerated.

A visual prosthetic would not solve every dimension of ADHD.
It would not fix sleep, emotional dysregulation, under-stimulation, shame loops, avoidance, or deeper executive dysfunction by itself.

But that is not a weakness of the idea.
It is exactly why the frame is useful.

A good prosthetic does not need to solve everything.
It only needs to reliably support one broken part of the loop.

And if that broken part is **priority persistence**, then the leverage may be enormous.

---

## Why this matters now

AR hardware is often marketed through entertainment, novelty, or generic future-tech language.

But one of the strongest near-term categories may be cognitive assistance.
Not in the abstract.
Not as hype.
But in practical, narrow, repeatable use cases.

ADHD is one of the clearest candidates because:

- the problem is frequent
- the problem is daily
- the problem is experiential and felt
- the problem is partly perceptual and contextual
- the intervention can be minimal but continuous

This makes AR less like a toy and more like interface infrastructure for cognition.

---

## Product direction

A serious prototype would likely need to be:

- minimal rather than immersive
- persistent rather than flashy
- state-aware rather than static
- corrective rather than distracting
- personalized rather than generic

The wrong product would feel like more noise.
The right product would feel like **a calm second layer of executive function**.

That is the design challenge.

---

## Final claim

The real promise of AR for ADHD may not be information access.
It may be **objective persistence**.

Not more data.
Not more stimulation.
Not another app.

A perceptual support layer that keeps the right thing alive in the line of sight when the mind would otherwise drop it.

That is why AR glasses may become a genuine visual prosthetic for some people with ADHD.

And if that is true, then the category is much bigger than smart glasses.

It is cognitive infrastructure.
